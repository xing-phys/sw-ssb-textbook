# Editorial and verification plan

Title: Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking: From Fundamentals to Research Frontiers.
Audience: senior undergraduates and beginning graduate students.
Literature cutoff: 22 October 2026 (not the unelapsed remainder of October).

## Logically ordered contents

### I. Foundations
1. Quantum language: vectors, operators, tensor products, density matrices, partial traces, entanglement.
2. Statistical mechanics and conventional spontaneous symmetry breaking: ensembles, thermodynamic limits, Ising model, Landau and fluctuations.
3. Open systems: Kraus maps, Stinespring dilation, measurement records, Lindblad generators and mixing.
4. Information and distinguishability: entropy, relative entropy, fidelity, mutual/conditional mutual information, recovery.

### II. Strong-to-weak order
5. Symmetry of states and dynamics: support theorem, charge sectors, covariance, non-Abelian caveats.
6. Nonlinear diagnostics: fidelity, Renyi-1, Renyi-2; inequalities, counterexamples, state versus phase.
7. Purification and doubled-space order: canonical versus Choi, environment and ensemble dependence.
8. Stability, locality and phases: proof architecture, local/global definitions, CMI, channel recoverability.
9. Solvable models and decoherence-driven transitions: parity ensembles, pair-error channels, Nishimori mapping, exact 1D and 2D thresholds.

### III. Research connections
10. Error correction and topological order: stabilizers, syndromes, inference, gauging and coherent information.
11. Mixed-state SPT phases and anomalies: cluster model, strong/weak protection, boundary and strange diagnostics.
12. Higher-form and non-Abelian symmetry: loops, anomalies, intrinsic mixed order, fusion/decoding hierarchy.
13. Hydrodynamics: continuity, diffusion, doubled symmetry, Goldstone description, finite-time versus steady-state order.
14. Charge scrambling and learning: fluctuations, skew information, conditional implications, sharpening and records.
15. Information-critical phases and frontier transitions: clock models, BKT, partial logical memory, continuous symmetries and long-range channels.
16. Detection and experiments: copy measurements, Renyi-1 resources, dephased Fermi gas, superconducting processors and finite-size claims.

### IV. Practice and research
17. Computational laboratory: reproducible Julia exact calculations, enumeration, plots and finite-size interpretation.
18. Research problems and synthesis: theorem/numerics/conjecture ledger and concrete projects.
19. Literature map: concept-indexed annotated primary-source coverage and cutoff audit.
Appendices: mathematical toolkit; glossary and notation; integrated bibliography.

Every substantive chapter includes motivation, explicitly defined prerequisites, derivations/worked examples, limitations, exercises and worked solutions. Specialized research constructions must state assumptions; no unsupported universal equivalences.
