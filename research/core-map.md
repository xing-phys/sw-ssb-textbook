## Theorem-assumption ledger

### Strong state symmetry

For positive finite-dimensional ρ, Uρ=χρ iff U is scalar χ on supp ρ. Every nonzero-weight vector in every convex decomposition lies in that support. This is independent of ensemble choice. For a non-Abelian group, the same scalar condition for all elements restricts to a one-dimensional character; a definite higher-dimensional irrep is insufficient. For SU(2), the support must be singlet, though multiplicities can be large.

### Strong channel symmetry

Use the sector-preserving convention [K_a,U_g]=0 for all Kraus operators, or a gatewise Stinespring dilation commuting with U_g⊗I_E. Merely conserving combined system-plus-bath charge allows exchange and yields only covariance. Some literature conventions allow common nonzero Kraus charges; the book fixes the zero-charge convention in the theorem.

### Finite-depth stability

Lessa et al. Theorem 1: finite local dimensions, bounded local supports/ranges, product ancillary inputs, strongly symmetric finite-depth local dilation. Output order parameter may be dressed and need not equal the original microscopic insertion. Proof uses Uhlmann and finite physical–ancilla operator expansion, not DPI alone. Absence of ordinary charged LRO is retained by disjoint backward cones. Growing-depth extension only guarantees exp[−poly(D)] lower bounds; at polylogarithmic depth these may vanish with volume.

### Rényi-1 equivalence

For normalized states with σ=AρA† and unitary A, affinity R1=Tr√ρ√σ obeys F²≤R1≤F. Chapter 6 gives a direct two-step Hilbert–Schmidt proof. For general nonunitary A the square-root conjugation identity fails, and normalized/postselected insertion probabilities must be distinguished. No generic unitary argument is asserted for ladder operators.

### Fidelity susceptibility and information geometry

Chapter 6 now reconstructs arXiv:2409.18944 with its **squared** Uhlmann-fidelity convention translated explicitly. For Hermitian charged Ising probes, define M2(p)=N^{-1}Σ_i F_root(ρ_p,Z_iρ_pZ_i)^2 and χ_N=∂_pM2(0+) at fixed N. Orthogonal parity blocks give χ_N/4=N F_root(ρ,σbar_i)^2 under translation invariance, where σbar_i=N^{-1}Σ_jZ_iZ_jρZ_jZ_i. The factor four is specific to the two matching Ising blocks. The bounds are Σ_j f_ij²≤χ_N/4≤min{N,(Σ_j f_ij)²}; lower bound derived by optimized Uhlmann flag amplitudes, upper by block-row trace-norm triangle inequality. Root one-point fidelity itself is O(√p) even for an unbroken paramagnet, so its derivative with respect to p is not the finite susceptibility claimed for unordered phases. Exact checks: paramagnet χ=4, parity ensemble χ=4N. Critical bounds alone do not establish a unique sublinear response exponent. Bures polarization with p_j=sin²θ_j gives Euclidean versus radial overlap geometry at the two fixed points. Reproducible independent noncommuting-state checks added to `numerics/core_checks.jl`.

### Local irrecoverability and CMI

Global fidelity LRO plus strong finite-volume symmetry implies some local symmetry-breaking channel has order-one unrecoverable error for every local recovery region not reaching the remote probe. The Pauli proof supplies an explicit trace-distance bound. Combining with approximate recovery forces nonzero complete-system CMI as buffer grows in the thermodynamic sequence. This does not assert that every high-CMI state has the same obstruction.

Inside an ordered phase, recovery of a symmetry-preserving deformation is controlled by **CMI loss** I_initial−I_final, not necessarily CMI itself (Lessa Sec. IV equation 85). A preserved global-charge plateau cancels. This resolves an apparent contradiction between ordered-state CMI and within-phase recoverability.

### Local/infinite-volume order

For nested regions R, f_R=F(ρ_R,O_xρ_RO_x†) decreases and has a limit. Global order implies positive local limit; converse is false (ETH pure-state example). Positive finite-region f_R is only an upper bound on the limit. Local tomography probes O(log N) volumes with polynomial-in-N resources, not arbitrary thermodynamic convergence.

The parity family and unrestricted maximally mixed family have identical local thermodynamic limits. Global fidelity distinguishes the finite-size families; intrinsic local definitions cannot. Liu–Yi–Else define strong symmetry by every extension and prove equivalence to vanishing charged local fidelity for compact groups. They regard absence of that symmetry as the relevant infinite-state property, explicitly questioning global-family-based phase distinctions. Their framework and global fidelity-based phase theory answer different operational questions; no blanket equivalence is asserted.

Divi et al. Sec. III.1 explicitly distinguishes finite-volume complete-system CMI from intrinsic local infinite-volume CMI. Their long-range infinite-volume statement concerns symmetry-averaged sector CMI **with a local-indistinguishability assumption**. Ordinary CMI of the infinite product state is zero. Liu et al. show CMI eventually vanishes for fixed bounded A and an exhausting buffer, without a universal decay-rate bound.

## Exact model reconstruction and independent checks

Chapter 9 derives P(n)=Σ_{∂s=n}p^{|s|}(1−p)^{E−|s|}. On a tree ∂ is injective. The open-chain formulas are F=R1=[2√p(1−p)]^r and R2=[2p(1−p)/(p²+(1−p)²)]^r. Only p=1/2 orders in one dimension for nearest-neighbor pair errors. A ring requires summing complementary strings; one must not reuse an open-chain finite-size formula blindly.

The planar cycle expansion gives RBIM coupling K=(1/2)ln[(1−p)/p], disorder probability p, and fidelity equal to the disorder half-moment of an endpoint-defect partition-function ratio. Nishimori p_c≈0.109 is numerical. A mean domain-wall argument alone does not prove decay: the exact first-moment identity E exp(−ΔF)=1 shows that rare disorder samples matter.

Separately, a Z-basis derivation maps purity and R2 to a clean Ising model with K2=−ln(1−2p). On the square lattice p_c^(2)=[1−√(√2−1)]/2≈0.178203 is exact given the clean critical coupling. Its Ising spin exponent is 1/4 and ν=1. The fidelity boundary and the Rényi-2 singularity are distinct; within-phase symmetric recovery can cross the latter.

It compares full density-matrix pair-channel calculations against the independent tree formulas and clean-Ising enumeration, checks noncommuting random-state affinity inequalities, and verifies the closed-form spectral counterexample. It is a genuine independent mathematical consistency check rather than an implementation-mirroring unit test.

## Editorial cautions

- Global SW-SSB excludes pure states; local order can include ETH pure states.
- Strong symmetry at finite volume is an exact support property, not an equation literally violated by spontaneous order.
- Canonical purification has marginal ρ. Normalized vectorization of ρ has marginal ρ²/Trρ².
- An efficient mixed-state preparation need not efficiently prepare its canonical purification. The parity Bell-cat example proves the distinction under geometric locality.
- A polynomial-in-ρ expression does not imply polynomial measurement complexity; exponentially small purity can make normalized ratios expensive.
- The early core paper's broad optimistic classical-shadow accessibility statement should be qualified by the later hardness result.
- Finite Markov length, finite correlation length, two-way channels, locally reversible channels, and infinite-state equivalence are different notions. Cite the exact criterion when labeling phases.
- The review contains “to appear” references without public primary records. They are research leads, not verified discoveries to be credited as published results.
